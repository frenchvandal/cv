A short note, written for my English-speaking readers.

Here is a rule I keep: write the site in the reader’s language first. The
English page starts life in English — its own sentences, its own jokes, its own
pace. It is not the French page wearing English clothes.

You can always tell when it is. A translated text betrays itself in inches:
sentences that run a beat too long, a "moreover" arriving a word too late, an
example set in a country the reader has never lived in. Nothing is false;
nothing is quite true either. Like a cover song played slightly off tempo.

Writing in the language first changes the work itself. I reach for examples that
live here — Shakespeare, the Tube, lunch as a quick refuelling stop rather than
a two-hour institution. I cut sentences where English wants them cut, short and
early. And when an English word has no French twin, I take it as proof that the
two versions are genuinely two texts, not a text and its shadow.

Is it more work? Yes. Every idea gets thought twice, not typed twice. But you,
the reader, owe me nothing — least of all the effort of guessing what the
sentence meant before someone translated it.
